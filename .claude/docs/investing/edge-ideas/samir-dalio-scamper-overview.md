## Key Themes Across the 20 Ideas

**1. Reactive, not predictive**  
None require forecasting precise returns. All react to observable state changes (debt-cycle phase, regime shifts, sentiment extremes, cross-asset confirmation).[1][2][3]

**2. Multi-year time horizons**  
Varma explicitly moved to >1 year to escape alpha decay and competition; Dalio's debt cycles unfold over 5-80 years.[2][4][1]

**3. Risk-first architecture**  
Several ideas (e.g., #16 "Risk State First, Signal Second") enforce that risk classification happens *before* signal generation—pure Varma.[5][6][1]

**4. Cross-domain borrowing**  
Control theory (PID controllers), martial arts (kuzushi, wu wei), cybersecurity (incident playbooks), chaos theory (attractors/constraints)—all adapted to markets.[7][8][9][10]

**5. Exploiting structural behavior, not news**  
Ideas like #5 (institutional liquidity preference) and #14 (inverse institutional flow) exploit documented, persistent biases that institutions cannot easily fix due to mandate/liquidity constraints.[11]

**6. No need for causal explanation**  
Many patterns (e.g., #4 sentiment × anomaly filter, #11 round-number secular inflections) don't require understanding *why*—just that they repeat statistically. Echoes Varma's Renaissance-style "illogical patterns" and Dalio's empirical cycle mapping.[1][2]

## A Few Standouts Worth Highlighting

#1: Debt-Cycle Phase Detection → Equity Exposure Sizing  
Pure Dalio cycle logic + Varma's regime-based sizing. Debt cycles are slow (5-10 years per phase), giving multi-year lead time to adjust.[3][12][13][2]

#3: 200DMA Risk Asymmetry × Debt Burden Composite  
Combines Varma's empirical 200DMA insight (2/3 return, 1/3 risk above; inverse below) with Dalio's debt-burden metrics. Creates a 2×2 or 3×3 risk matrix with decade-scale persistence.[2][1]

#5: Institutional Liquidity Preference × Multi-Year Holding Arb  
Research explicitly shows institutions trade *against* long-horizon anomalies (value, quality) because those stocks are illiquid. You can systematically exploit this by holding what they're forced to avoid.[11]

#7: Kuzushi (Off-Balance) Detection in Crowded Themes  
Martial arts principle applied to bubble detection. Themes (AI, crypto, cannabis, SPACs) take 2-3 years to build and 6-18 months to crack. Detecting the "off-balance" moment (breadth divergence, parabolic exhaustion) sets up high-R shorts.[8][9]

#12: Zero-Forecasting Multi-Year Trend Portfolio  
Eliminates all return models. Pure reactive: trend (200+ day MA) + debt-cycle phase + cross-asset confirmation. If 3/3 bullish, 100% long; 2/3 → 50%; 1/3 → flat. Rebalance quarterly, hold for years. No alpha, pure regime response.[14][15][1]

#14: Inverse Institutional Flow on Long-Horizon Anomalies  
Research shows institutions systematically sell illiquid value and buy liquid growth due to structural constraints, creating a persistent mispricing that takes 3-5 years to correct. Build a strategy that does the opposite.[11]

#16: Rearrange: Risk State First, Signal Second  
Reverses the typical "scan → filter risk" flow to "compute risk → scan only if allowed." In high-risk regimes, don't even look for trades (wu wei). Forces discipline and eliminates the temptation to "force" trades when conditions are bad.[16][17][1]

#18: Cyber Incident Playbook for Market Dislocations  
Borrows from cybersecurity incident response: pre-define "market incidents" (flash crash, vol spike, correlation surge) and exact playbooks (cut to X%, widen stops, suspend strategies Y and Z). Removes emotion, which Varma emphasizes is critical during drawdowns.[18][1]

#20: No Trades in Neutral Regimes (Shi Absence)  
When all your indicators are mid-range (trend neutral, vol normal, sentiment balanced, debt-cycle mid-phase), you have no positional advantage (shi). Wu wei dictates: do nothing. Only trade when extremes create clear advantage.[19][8][16]
