### Ray Dalio's Framework Recap

Dalio's long-term debt cycle model identifies **five phases**:[2][3][8]

1. **Phase 1 (Early Expansion):** Low debt, high capacity for borrowing, strong growth
2. **Phase 2 (Mid-Cycle):** Debt grows faster than income but remains manageable, steady growth
3. **Phase 3 (Late Cycle/Bubble):** Debt/GDP reaches historical highs, asset prices elevated, monetary policy tightening begins limiting effectiveness
4. **Phase 4 (Top/Deleveraging Begins):** Debt servicing strains emerge, defaults rise, credit spreads widen, central banks lose policy effectiveness ("pushing on a string")
5. **Phase 5 (Depression/Restructuring):** Widespread defaults, debt restructurings, asset liquidation, monetary/fiscal coordination required

**Exploitable behavior:**

  - Calculate **implied equity vol** from CDS spreads using Merton model
  - Compare to **realized equity vol** (30-day trailing)

  - If realized vol > implied vol by >5 vol points → equity rich, credit cheap
- **Trade construction (Example: Equity cheap):**
  - Long equity call options (6-month ATM)
  - Short CDS (5-year tenor) to hedge credit risk
  - Size to delta-neutral (adjust for option delta and CDS DV01)

  - Long CDS protection
  - Short equity put options

  - Short equity to delta-neutral (hedge equity risk)

  - Result: exposure only to **credit spread** and **volatility**; profit when bond price converges to fair value
- **Position sizing:** Risk-parity based on credit spread and equity vol
- **Rebalance hedges:** Daily (equity delta changes as stock moves)

**Expected performance:**
- **2022-2023:** Rate/credit/equity vol all elevated; convertible arb Sharpe ~1.8 as dislocations abundant[43]
- **Historical:** Sharpe 1.2-1.5 in calm markets; 1.6-2.0+ during transitions[43]

**Capacity:** $1-3B (limited by convertible bond market size ~$400B globally)

**Complexity:** Requires **options pricing expertise**, **multi-asset hedging**, and **daily risk management**—institutional quality infrastructure needed.

### **Defensive & Tail-Risk Strategies**

These strategies provide **convexity** (nonlinear payoffs) and **crisis alpha** during Stage 4 deleveraging.

#### **Strategy 13: Equity Put Spread Collars + Cash Redeployment**

**Cycle Stages:** 3-4  
**Timeframe:** 1-3 years  
**Expected Sharpe:** 0.8-1.3  
**Impact:** Medium | **Effort:** Medium  
**MoSCoW:** **Should Have**

**Structural rationale:**
Put spread collars combine **downside protection** (long put) with **premium financing** (short call + short lower-strike put), resulting in **low net cost** tail hedging. During late-cycle, this structure **caps upside** but **preserves capital** during crashes, enabling **opportunistic redeployment** at depressed valuations.[44][45][46]

**Exploitable behavior:**
- **Volatility smile:** Out-of-the-money puts are overpriced (implied vol > realized); selling them funds protection[44]
- **Behavioral selling:** Retail panic creates forced liquidations, allowing hedged portfolios to buy at bottoms[24][14]

**Implementation:**
- **Core equity portfolio:** 80% SPY long position
- **Collar structure (per $100 notional):**
  - Buy SPY **put** at 90% strike (10% downside protection)
  - Sell SPY **call** at 110% strike (cap upside at 10%)
  - Sell SPY **put** at 80% strike (reduce net cost)
  - Net cost: ~0.5-1.0% of notional annually (vs 2-3% for naked puts)
- **Rolling:** Quarterly roll to maintain protection
- **Redeployment rule:** If SPY falls >15%, liquidate put spread (lock in gain), deploy proceeds into defensive sectors or distressed opportunities

**Expected performance:**
- **2008 analog:** Collar limited losses to -10% while SPY fell -38%; redeployment at bottom turned into +60% gain by 2010[46][44]
- **Sharpe:** 0.8-1.0 in bull markets (upside capped); 1.2-1.5 over full cycle (crash protection + redeployment)[45][44]

**Capacity:** $10B+ (SPY options highly liquid)

#### **Strategy 19: Short High-Leverage Zombie Companies (via CDS or Put Options)**

**Cycle Stages:** 4-5  
**Timeframe:** 1-2 years  
**Expected Sharpe:** 1.0-2.0  
**Impact:** Medium | **Effort:** Medium  
**MoSCoW:** **Could Have** (timing dependent on Stage 4 onset)

**Structural rationale:**
**Zombie companies** (firms with interest coverage ratio <1.0—unable to cover interest expense from EBITDA) survive in low-rate environments but face **refinancing cliffs** when rates rise or credit tightens. Shorting zombies during Stage 3→4 transition captures **forced deleveraging, bankruptcy, or equity dilution**.[11][10]

**Exploitable behavior:**
- **Extend-and-pretend:** Banks roll zombie loans to avoid recognizing losses; eventually exhausted[10]
- **Retail HODLing:** Retail investors hold zombie stocks hoping for turnaround, ignoring deteriorating fundamentals[24]

**Implementation:**
- **Screen for zombies:**
  - Interest coverage ratio (EBIT / Interest Expense) < 1.0 for 2+ consecutive years
  - Debt-to-equity > 2.0
  - Negative free cash flow
  - Upcoming debt maturity within 18 months (refinancing risk)
- **Short vehicle:**
  - **CDS protection** (5-year tenor) if available (better capital efficiency)
  - **Equity put options** (6-12 month, 20% OTM) if CDS unavailable
- **Portfolio construction:** Equal-weight basket of 10-15 zombies
- **Stop-loss:** Exit if company announces equity raise + debt restructuring (dilutes but extends runway)

**Expected performance:**
- **2008:** Zombie equities fell -60 to -90% as credit dried up; CDS paid 40-60 points[11][10]
- **Sharpe:** Highly skewed; 1.0-1.5 if timed correctly (Stage 4 onset); 0.0-0.5 if early (zombie survival in benign credit)[11]

**Capacity:** $500M-$1B (limited by CDS/options liquidity)

**Timing:** Deploy when **high-yield default rate >5%** OR **credit spreads >500 bps**.[1][15]

**2025 status:** Premature; default rates low. **Wait for Stage 4 catalyst**.[15][1]

#### **Strategy 20: Tail-Risk Hedging via VIX Calls + Systematic Rebalancing**

**Cycle Stages:** 3-4-5  
**Timeframe:** 1-3 years  
**Expected Sharpe:** 0.5-1.5  
**Impact:** Medium | **Effort:** Medium  
**MoSCoW:** **Should Have**

**Structural rationale:**
VIX call options provide **convex payoffs** during volatility spikes (1-3 month VIX futures calls can return 5-10x during crashes). By **systematically rolling** VIX calls (1-2% of portfolio monthly) and **rebalancing gains** into risk assets, portfolios reduce drawdowns by 10-15% while maintaining long-term returns.[45][46][44]

**Exploitable behavior:**
- **Volatility risk premium:** VIX futures trade in contango (future > spot) 80% of the time; selling premium to finance tail hedges[44][45]
- **Panic vol spikes:** VIX routinely spikes to 40-80 during crises (from baseline 12-15), creating option payoffs[44]

**Implementation:**
- **Core portfolio:** 95% SPY long
- **Tail hedge (5% notional):**
  - Buy VIX **call options** 3-month tenor, 25-30 strike (OTM by ~10 points)
  - Roll monthly (sell expiring calls, buy new 3-month)
  - Average cost: ~0.3-0.5% of portfolio per month = 3.6-6% annually
- **Rebalancing rule:**
  - If VIX calls return >3x (VIX spikes >30), sell 50% of calls
  - Redeploy proceeds into SPY or undervalued sectors
  - Maintain 5% hedge allocation
- **Financing:** Fund hedge by **shorting VIX futures** at back end of curve (6-9 month tenor) to capture contango decay (~1-2% monthly)

**Expected performance:**
- **2008:** VIX spiked to 80; 30-strike calls returned 15-20x; portfolio drawdown -25% vs -38% unhedged[45][44]
- **2020 COVID:** VIX spiked to 82; calls returned 10-15x; redeployment at March lows yielded +40% gain by year-end[45][44]
- **Full-cycle Sharpe:** 0.5-0.8 due to hedge drag in bull markets; 1.2-1.5+ including redeployment alpha[44][45]

**Capacity:** $5B+ (VIX options liquid)

#### **Strategy 10: Short Duration High-Yield with Selective CDS Hedges**

**Exploitable behavior:**

**Expected performance:**
- **Bull case (no recession):** SJNK yields 5-6%; minus CDS cost (0.5-1.0%) = net carry 4-5%; Sharpe ~1.2[49]
- **Bear case (recession):** Hedged credits default, CDS pays out 40-60 points; unhedged credits fall 10-20%; net loss -5 to -10% vs -30% for unhedged HY; Sharpe ~0.9[15]

**Capacity:** $1-3B

**2025 status:** HY spreads tight (OAS ~350 bps); **marginal risk-reward**. Consider waiting for spread widening to >450 bps.[1][15]
