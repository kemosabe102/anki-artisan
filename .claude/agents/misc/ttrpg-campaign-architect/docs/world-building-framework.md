# World-Building Framework for TTRPG Campaigns

**Purpose**: Structured approach to creating coherent, playable game worlds

---

## The Iceberg Principle

**What Players See (10%)**: Immediate details - the tavern, the guard captain, today's weather

**What You Prepare (30%)**: One step behind the curtain - faction motivations, regional politics, active plots

**What You Sketch (60%)**: Broad strokes only - ancient history, distant lands, cosmic truths

**Rule**: Only detail what players will interact with. Everything else stays flexible.

---

## World Element Taxonomy

### 1. Geography & Setting

**Questions to Answer**:
- What defines this world's physical reality?
- How do people travel and communicate?
- What natural resources create conflict?

**Elements to Define**:
- **Regions**: Broad areas with distinct character
- **Landmarks**: Memorable locations for adventure
- **Routes**: How things and people move
- **Hazards**: Environmental dangers and obstacles

**GM Utility Focus**: 
- Describe in terms of travel time, not miles
- Include sensory details (sounds, smells, temperature)
- Note what makes each location memorable for players

### 2. Creatures & Species

**Questions to Answer**:
- What intelligent species exist and how do they interact?
- What creatures threaten travelers and settlements?
- What animals/beasts are common or prized?



**Elements to Define**:
- **Playable Species**: Cultures, tensions, stereotypes to subvert
- **Monsters**: Threat level, habitat, behaviors
- **Wildlife**: Flavor and utility (mounts, food, materials)

**GM Utility Focus**:
- Include behavioral patterns (how do they react to parties?)
- Note unique traits that create interesting encounters
- Connect to factions or geography where relevant

### 3. Factions & Organizations

**Questions to Answer**:
- Who holds power and how do they maintain it?
- What groups compete for resources or influence?
- Where do conflicts and alliances form?

**Elements to Define**:
- **Goals**: What does the faction want? (1-3 clear objectives)
- **Resources**: What do they have to achieve goals?
- **Key Figures**: 2-3 named NPCs with brief descriptions
- **Relationships**: Allies, enemies, neutral parties

**GM Utility Focus**:
- Express goals as actionable objectives
- Include "what they'd ask adventurers to do"
- Note points of tension that create plot hooks

### 4. Culture & Society

**Questions to Answer**:
- How do people in this world live day-to-day?
- What do they value, celebrate, fear?
- What creates social conflict or cohesion?

**Elements to Define**:
- **Values**: What matters to this culture? (honor, wealth, family, knowledge)
- **Customs**: Distinctive practices players will encounter
- **Taboos**: Things forbidden that create tension
- **Social Structure**: Who has status and why?

**GM Utility Focus**:
- Include customs that create roleplay opportunities
- Note taboos PCs might accidentally violate
- Connect to available plot hooks

### 5. Magic & Special Rules

**Questions to Answer**:
- What's possible that isn't in the real world?
- What limits or costs exist on special powers?
- How does this affect society and conflict?

**Elements to Define**:
- **Source**: Where does magic/power come from?
- **Costs**: What's the price for using it?
- **Limits**: What can't it do?
- **Social Impact**: How does society view/use it?

**GM Utility Focus**:
- Keep rules simple and memorable
- Focus on story implications over mechanics
- Include plot hooks related to magic's costs/limits

## World Coherence Principles

### Internal Consistency

**Rule**: If X is true, what else must be true?

- If teleportation exists → Why do roads matter?
- If undead are common → How do burial customs change?
- If dragons hoard gold → What's the economic impact?

**Test**: Can players predict logical consequences?

### The "Yes, And" Foundation

Every world element should support play:
- **Adventurer Economy**: Why do adventurers exist and get paid?
- **Conflict Abundance**: Where do problems needing heroes come from?
- **Information Flow**: How do people learn about opportunities?

### Sandbox vs. Railroad Balance

**Sandbox Elements** (player-driven):
- Multiple active factions with goals
- Rumors pointing to optional content
- Consequences that create new situations

**Narrative Elements** (GM-driven):
- Central conflict that escalates
- Key revelations at story beats
- Climactic confrontation setup

**Best Practice**: Sandbox world, narrative stakes

---

## Quick Reference: World Element Checklist

- [ ] Geography supports adventure (travel, exploration, danger)
- [ ] Factions have clear goals and conflicts with each other
- [ ] Creatures fit logically into ecosystems and societies
- [ ] Culture creates roleplay opportunities and tensions
- [ ] Magic/special rules have clear costs and limits
- [ ] Elements connect to each other (factions use creatures, geography shapes culture)
- [ ] Room exists for player-driven exploration
- [ ] Central conflicts provide narrative backbone
