# Storytelling Fundamentals for TTRPG Campaigns

**Purpose**: Core narrative principles for creating engaging tabletop RPG stories

---

## What Makes a Good Story?

### The Four Pillars

1. **Conflict** - The engine that drives the narrative forward
2. **Stakes** - What's at risk if the conflict isn't resolved
3. **Agency** - Player choices that matter and shape outcomes
4. **Consequence** - Actions have meaningful results (positive and negative)

### Conflict Types (Man vs...)

| Type | Description | TTRPG Application |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Nature** | Survival, environment, beasts | Wilderness travel, natural disasters, creature encounters |
| **Society** | Laws, customs, expectations | Political intrigue, social climbing, cultural clashes |
| **Others** | Direct opposition, rivals | Villain factions, competing parties, personal enemies |
| **Self** | Internal struggle, moral dilemma | Character backstory, corruption arcs, redemption quests |
| **Supernatural** | Forces beyond mortal understanding | Gods, curses, prophecies, cosmic horror |
| **Technology/Magic** | Unintended consequences | Magical corruption, artifacts with costs, forbidden knowledge |

## Story Structure for Campaigns

### Three-Act Framework (Adapted for TTRPG)

**Act 1: Setup (Early Game)**
- Introduce the world and its rules
- Establish player characters in the setting
- Present the inciting incident (the hook)
- Plant seeds for later revelations

**Act 2: Confrontation (Mid Game)**
- Escalate complications and stakes
- Test character bonds and beliefs
- Reveal hidden truths and betrayals
- Build toward a point of no return

**Act 3: Resolution (End Game)**
- Climactic confrontation with central conflict
- Character arcs reach conclusion
- World changes based on outcomes
- Seeds for future stories (if continuing)

## Revelation Timing Strategy

**Core Principle**: Place your best ideas strategically for maximum impact.

> **Best idea → END** (the payoff players remember)
> **Second-best → EARLY** (the hook that makes them care)
> **Third-best → MIDDLE** (prevents the "saggy middle")

### Why This Order?

| Placement | Idea Rank | Purpose |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **End Game** | #1 Best | The climax is what players remember. Save your most impactful revelation, twist, or confrontation for the finale. |
| **Early Game** | #2 Second-best | You need something strong to hook players immediately. A weak opening means they never reach your best idea. |
| **Mid Game** | #3 Third-best | The middle is where campaigns often sag. A solid mid-game reveal keeps momentum when initial excitement fades. |
| **Throughout** | #4-6 | Supporting beats, character moments, and world-building distributed as needed. |

### The Ranking Process

**Step 1: Generate Ideas** (brainstorm freely)
- List 6-8 story concepts: twists, reveals, confrontations, character moments
- Don't worry about timing yet — just get them out

**Step 2: Rank by Impact**
- Ask: "Which would players talk about for months?"
- Consider: Emotional punch, surprise factor, thematic resonance
- Agent proposes ranking with rationale → User confirms or reorders

**Step 3: Strategic Placement**
- Rank 1 → End Game climax
- Rank 2 → Early Game hook
- Rank 3 → Mid Game escalation
- Ranks 4-6 → Supporting beats across phases

### Example Application

**Ideas generated**:
1. The mentor was secretly the BBEG all along
2. The "cursed" artifact is actually trying to help
3. The safe haven city is controlled by the cult
4. Former ally returns seeking revenge
5. The prophecy was mistranslated
6. The real villain is the hero's future self

**Ranked by impact**:
- **#1**: Mentor is BBEG (maximum betrayal payoff)
- **#2**: Artifact is helpful (immediate intrigue, reframes the quest)
- **#3**: City is compromised (mid-game stakes explosion)
- **#4-6**: Remaining ideas for supporting beats

**Placed strategically**:
- **Early Game**: Artifact seems cursed but... hints it's more complex
- **Mid Game**: Safe city revealed as trap — nowhere is safe
- **End Game**: Mentor unmasked as the true threat



### Pacing Principles

**Tension Curve**: Not constant high action - vary intensity
- **Peaks**: Combat, revelations, dramatic confrontations
- **Valleys**: Roleplay, exploration, planning, downtime
- **Rising Action**: Build toward peaks with mounting stakes
- **Release**: Moments of victory, humor, connection after tension

**Session Rhythm**:
- Open with hook or reminder of stakes
- Build through middle with choices and discoveries
- End on a cliffhanger or emotional beat

## Character-Driven Story Elements

### Motivation Types

| Type | Description | Plot Hook Potential |
|------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Survival** | Stay alive, protect loved ones | Threat to safety, resources |
| **Power** | Gain influence, strength, control | Opportunities, rivals, costs |
| **Knowledge** | Understand mysteries, learn truths | Secrets, forbidden lore, mentors |
| **Belonging** | Find community, earn acceptance | Factions, outcasts, chosen families |
| **Justice** | Right wrongs, enforce fairness | Crimes, corruption, old debts |
| **Legacy** | Be remembered, build something lasting | Founding, creation, sacrifice |

### Creating Meaningful Choices

**Good Choices Have**:
- Clear stakes (players know what's at risk)
- No obvious "right" answer (trade-offs exist)
- Lasting consequences (world remembers)
- Character relevance (connects to who they are)

**Avoid**:
- False choices (all paths lead same place)
- Punishment for "wrong" answers
- Choices without information to decide
- Stakes that don't matter to characters

## Plot Point Design Principles

### The Hook Formula

Every plot hook should answer:
1. **What** - The immediate situation or event
2. **Why It Matters** - Stakes and consequences
3. **Options** - At least 2-3 response paths
4. **Connections** - Links to world/character elements

### Positive/Negative/Neutral Typing

**Positive Plots**: Opportunities, rewards, allies appearing
- Use to: Balance dark tones, reward players, introduce helpful NPCs

**Negative Plots**: Threats, losses, enemies acting
- Use to: Raise stakes, create urgency, test characters

**Neutral Plots**: Choices without clear good/bad, mysteries, changes
- Use to: Add complexity, present dilemmas, introduce ambiguity

**Balance Guide**: 
- Early game: 40% positive, 30% negative, 30% neutral
- Mid game: 25% positive, 50% negative, 25% neutral  
- End game: 30% positive, 40% negative, 30% neutral

## Quick Reference: Story Quality Checklist

- [ ] Is there clear conflict driving the narrative?
- [ ] Do stakes exist that players care about?
- [ ] Can player choices meaningfully affect outcomes?
- [ ] Are consequences visible and consistent?
- [ ] Does pacing vary between tension and release?
- [ ] Are multiple conflict types represented?
- [ ] Do plot hooks connect to character motivations?
- [ ] Is there room for player-driven direction?

## Campaign Review: Disney Creative Strategy

**Purpose**: Quick validation of completed campaigns using three creative lenses.

### The Three Roles

| Role | Question | Focus |
|------|----------|-------|
| **🌟 Dreamer** | "Does this inspire wonder?" | Vision, excitement, memorable moments |
| **🔧 Realist** | "Can this actually be run?" | Practicality, session count, pacing |
| **🔍 Critic** | "What could go wrong?" | Plot holes, inconsistencies, weak points |

### Quick Review Process (~30 seconds)

**Dreamer Check**:
- Are the stakes meaningful enough to care about?
- Is there at least one moment players will talk about after?
- Does the premise spark immediate interest?

**Realist Check**:
- Are there enough plot hooks for the session count?
- Can the GM actually run this with the information provided?
- Is the pacing achievable (not too rushed, not dragging)?

**Critic Check**:
- Any plot threads that go nowhere?
- Inconsistencies between world rules and story events?
- Is the BBEG motivation clear enough to play?

### Review Output Format

```yaml
review:
  dreamer:
    verdict: pass | needs_work
    note: "[What inspires or what's missing]"
  realist:
    verdict: pass | needs_work
    note: "[What works or what's impractical]"
  critic:
    verdict: pass | needs_work
    note: "[What's solid or what has holes]"

  overall: ready | refine_recommended
  top_suggestion: "[Single highest-impact improvement]"
```

### When to Use

- After completing the Connections phase (automatic)
- When user says "review this" or "check this"
- Before final export
