# CEFR B1/B2 Vocabulary Deck Framework

A language-agnostic guide for creating pedagogically-sound vocabulary flashcard decks at the B1 (Intermediate) and B2 (Upper-Intermediate) levels, based on CEFR standards and language acquisition research.

---

## Overview

**What:** Framework for building B1/B2 vocabulary decks for any language
**Why:** B-level marks the transition from survival communication to independent, professional-capable language use
**How:** Progressive complexity, professional vocabulary integration, abstract concept development, and discourse marker mastery

---

## CEFR Word Count Targets

| Level | Cumulative Words | CEFR Description | Communication Goal |
|-------|------------------|------------------|-------------------|
| B1 | 2,000-3,000 | Threshold/Intermediate | Handle most travel situations, discuss familiar topics, express opinions |
| B2 | 4,000-5,000 | Vantage/Upper-Intermediate | Understand complex texts, engage in technical discussions, articulate nuanced arguments |

**Key distinction:** B1 vocabulary handles concrete, everyday communication with basic opinion expression. B2 vocabulary enables abstract discussion, professional argumentation, and nuanced expression across registers.

---

## B1 Level: Independent Communication

### Target Outcomes
At B1 completion, learners should be able to:
- Deal with most situations likely to arise while traveling
- Describe experiences, events, dreams, hopes, and ambitions
- Briefly give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans
- Produce simple connected text on familiar topics

### Core B1 Thematic Categories (7 domains)

B1 builds on A1/A2 foundations with increased depth and sophistication.

#### 1. Work and Employment
**Purpose:** Enable workplace conversations and job-related communication

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Job roles and titles (employee, manager, executive, intern)
- Workplace vocabulary (office, department, headquarters, branch)
- Employment terms (contract, salary, overtime, promotion, benefits)
- Contract types (full-time, part-time, fixed-term, permanent)
- Work actions (hire, fire, resign, promote, transfer, manage)
- Project vocabulary (deadline, goal, result, evaluation)

**Target:** ~50 words

#### 2. Media, Technology, and Communication
**Purpose:** Navigate digital environments and discuss media consumption

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Devices (computer, screen, keyboard, laptop, tablet)
- Digital actions (download, upload, save, delete, share, connect)
- Internet vocabulary (website, link, network, connection, password)
- Media types (newspaper, magazine, news broadcast, documentary, series)
- Tech verbs (update, install, restart, crash)

**Target:** ~50 words

#### 3. Travel, Services, and Transactions
**Purpose:** Handle complex travel situations and public services

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Travel logistics (reservation, confirmation, cancellation, delay, connection)
- Public services (counter, form, document, application, paperwork)
- Official documents (ID, passport, visa, permit)
- Banking basics (account, ATM, withdraw, deposit, transfer, balance)
- Consumer transactions (receipt, invoice, refund, return, warranty, complaint)

**Target:** ~50 words

#### 4. Health and Well-being
**Purpose:** Describe symptoms and navigate healthcare situations

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Medical process (symptom, diagnosis, treatment, therapy, checkup)
- Healthcare settings (emergency room, ward, pharmacy)
- Medications (prescription, medication, antibiotic, painkiller, tablet)
- Conditions (allergy, infection, inflammation, fracture, sprain)
- Symptoms (fever, pain, swelling, nausea, dizziness)
- Health verbs (recover, worsen, improve, treat, prescribe)

**Target:** ~50 words

#### 5. Education and Training
**Purpose:** Discuss educational experiences and learning environments

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Institutions (university, faculty, high school, institute)
- Academic terms (exam, grade, degree, diploma, course, thesis)
- Academic roles (student, professor, instructor, dean)
- Academic verbs (graduate, enroll, attend, pass, fail, review)
- Academic objects (classroom, library, notes, notebook)

**Target:** ~50 words

#### 6. Opinions, Emotions, and Personal Qualities
**Purpose:** Express thoughts, feelings, and describe personality

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Opinion expressions (in my opinion, I think that, I believe that)
- Agreement/disagreement (agree, disagree, share an opinion)
- Emotions (worried, anxious, enthusiastic, disappointed, frustrated)
- Personal qualities (honest, reliable, patient, generous, stubborn, ambitious)
- Personality types (optimistic, pessimistic, introverted, extroverted)

**Target:** ~50 words

#### 7. Basic Connectors and Discourse Markers
**Purpose:** Build coherent text and support complex sentence structures

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Additive (furthermore, also, in addition, moreover)
- Adversative (but, however, nevertheless, instead, on the contrary)
- Causal (because, since, given that, due to, thanks to)
- Result (therefore, so, consequently, for this reason)
- Sequential (first, then, after, finally, meanwhile)
- Conditional/Concessive (if, in case, unless, even if, despite)
- Explanation/Conclusion (that is, for example, in conclusion, to summarize)

**Target:** ~50 words

### Grammar Concepts B1 Vocabulary Must Support

B1 vocabulary should enable learners to use:
- Past tense distinctions (completed vs. habitual/ongoing)
- Introduction to subjunctive mood (where applicable)
- Conditional tense (hypotheticals, polite requests)
- Reported speech
- Passive voice (basic)
- Complex sentence structures with multiple clauses

## B2 Level: Professional and Academic Communication

### Target Outcomes
At B2 completion, learners should be able to:
- Understand the main ideas of complex text on concrete and abstract topics
- Interact with native speakers with fluency and spontaneity
- Produce clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects
- Explain a viewpoint giving advantages and disadvantages

**Note:** B2 is typically required for university admission in the target language country and represents professional working proficiency.

### Core B2 Thematic Categories (7 domains)

#### 1. Professional Communication and Formal Register
**Purpose:** Master formal correspondence and professional etiquette

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Formal salutations (Dear Sir/Madam, formal titles)
- Email/letter phrases (attached, in reference to, as per our discussion)
- Formal closings (kind regards, sincerely, respectfully)
- Polite request formulas (would you kindly, I would be grateful if)
- Register markers (formal vs. informal indicators)
- Professional verbs (confirm, request, forward, attach)

**Target:** ~50 words

#### 2. Business and Corporate Terminology
**Purpose:** Navigate corporate environments and business discussions

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Departments (Human Resources, Marketing, Sales, Accounting, Legal)
- Corporate hierarchy (CEO, Director, Manager, shareholder, board)
- Finance terms (revenue, budget, profit, loss, investment)
- Business processes (merger, acquisition, strategy, growth, competition)
- Meeting vocabulary (agenda, minutes, convene, propose, approve)
- Negotiation terms (negotiate, compromise, terms, conditions, clause)

**Target:** ~50 words

#### 3. Current Events, Society, and Institutions
**Purpose:** Engage with news and discuss social/political topics

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Political institutions (government, ministry, parliament, president)
- Legal vocabulary (court, law, decree, regulation, rights, duties)
- Social issues (society, citizen, immigration, unemployment, equality)
- Media vocabulary (article, editorial, investigation, report)
- Current issues (crisis, reform, protest, demonstration, debate)
- Civic verbs (vote, support, oppose, denounce)

**Target:** ~50 words

#### 4. Abstract Concepts and Intellectual Vocabulary
**Purpose:** Discuss abstract ideas and engage in sophisticated discourse

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Values (freedom, justice, equality, dignity, responsibility)
- Emotions/psychological (trust, courage, fear, hope, doubt, certainty)
- Intellectual concepts (knowledge, experience, behavior, attitude, meaning)
- Cause and effect (consequence, cause, effect, result, objective)
- Outcomes (success, failure, advantage, disadvantage, benefit, risk)
- Analytical verbs (reflect, analyze, evaluate, interpret, deduce, demonstrate)

**Target:** ~50 words

#### 5. Advanced Connectors and Argumentation
**Purpose:** Construct sophisticated arguments with nuanced logical connections

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Concessive (although, despite the fact that, even though, however much)
- Conditional (in case, on condition that, provided that, unless, assuming that)
- Result/consequence (therefore, consequently, it follows that, hence)
- Reformulation (that is to say, in other words, namely)
- Opinion markers (in my view, I believe that, from my perspective)
- Hedging (apparently, so to speak, in a sense)
- Contrast (on one hand... on the other, rather than, instead of)
- Conclusion (in conclusion, ultimately, to sum up, in light of)

**Target:** ~50 words

#### 6. Idioms and Idiomatic Expressions
**Purpose:** Achieve natural, culturally fluent expression

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Common everyday idioms
- Body-related idioms
- Food/object-related idioms
- Animal-related idioms
- Proverbs and sayings
- Fixed expressions

**Note:** Idioms are highly language-specific. Research the 30-50 most common idioms that B2 learners encounter.

**Target:** ~50 idioms/expressions

#### 7. Environment, Technology, and Science
**Purpose:** Discuss contemporary environmental and technological issues

**Vocabulary categories:**
- Environmental issues (pollution, recycling, sustainability, climate change)
- Ecology (ecosystem, biodiversity, conservation, deforestation)
- Energy/resources (renewable energy, emissions, carbon footprint)
- Technology (artificial intelligence, algorithm, software, privacy, cybersecurity)
- Science (research, discovery, experiment, hypothesis, theory)
- Action verbs (innovate, develop, protect, pollute, reduce, waste)

**Target:** ~50 words

### Grammar Concepts B2 Vocabulary Must Support

B2 vocabulary should enable learners to use:
- Full subjunctive usage (where applicable)
- Complex conditionals (hypothetical past, mixed conditions)
- Advanced passive constructions
- Nominalization (verb → noun transformations)
- Formal register throughout
- Nuanced tense/aspect distinctions
- Complex relative clauses
- Reported speech with backshift

## Professional Vocabulary Integration Strategy

Professional vocabulary should be distributed across levels rather than isolated:

| Level | Professional Content |
|-------|---------------------|
| A2 | Basic job titles, workplace basics |
| B1 | Employment terms, contracts, work processes |
| B1 | Basic discourse markers for meetings |
| B2 | Formal correspondence, email conventions |
| B2 | Corporate structure, negotiations, finance |

This mirrors how certification exams introduce professional vocabulary: basic workplace terms at B1, sophisticated business communication at B2.

The deck progression should explicitly support grammar development:

| Grammar Concept | Supporting Decks |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| Past tense distinctions | B1-3, B1-4 (temporal markers in context) |
| Introduction to conditional | B1-6 (opinion expressions with "would") |
| Basic subjunctive | B1-6 (think that, believe that), B2-1 (formal requests) |
| Full subjunctive usage | B2-4 (doubt/belief vocabulary), B2-5 (concessive connectors) |
| Complex conditionals | B2-5 (conditional markers) |
| Formal register | B2-1 (professional communication throughout) |

## Research-Backed Design Principles for B-Level

### 1. Abstract Vocabulary Development

B-level marks the shift from concrete to abstract vocabulary:

**Concrete (A1/A2):** table, eat, red, happy
**Abstract (B1/B2):** responsibility, consequence, hypothesis, despite

Abstract nouns require:
- Multiple example sentences showing usage
- Collocations (take responsibility, face consequences)
- Register notes (formal vs. informal contexts)

### 2. Collocation Emphasis

B-level vocabulary should emphasize word partnerships:

- Verb + noun (make a decision, take action, reach an agreement)
- Adjective + noun (renewable energy, artificial intelligence)
- Adverb + adjective (highly likely, deeply concerned)

Include common collocations in example sentences.

### 3. Register Awareness

B2 learners must navigate formal/informal register shifts:

- Mark vocabulary for register (formal, neutral, informal)
- Provide formal alternatives for informal expressions
- Note context-appropriate usage

### 4. Discourse Competence

Connected discourse distinguishes B-level from A-level:

- Prioritize connectors and discourse markers
- Show how vocabulary builds arguments
- Include transition phrases for writing/speaking

### 5. Idiomatic Competence

Idioms mark the difference between textbook fluency and natural expression:

- Select high-frequency, widely-understood idioms
- Provide literal translation for comprehension
- Include natural usage context
- Note register (some idioms are informal only)

## Language-Specific Adaptations for B-Level

### Formal Register Systems

Languages vary in how they encode formality:

- **Pronoun-based:** T-V distinction (tu/vous, du/Sie, tu/Lei)
- **Verb-based:** Honorific verb forms (Japanese, Korean)
- **Vocabulary-based:** Formal word choices (English)
- **Combined:** Multiple markers (Japanese keigo)

Document the target language's formality system and ensure B2 decks teach full formal register.

### Business/Professional Vocabulary

Some languages borrow heavily from English for business terms:

- Note which terms are loanwords vs. native vocabulary
- Include both if both are used
- Mark register differences (loanword may be more casual)

### Idiomatic Systems

Idioms vary dramatically by language:

- Research most common B2-level idioms for target language
- Include body-part idioms (usually universal pattern)
- Include food/animal idioms (culturally specific)
- Include weather idioms (vary by climate/culture)

### Connector Systems

Languages structure arguments differently:

- Some require explicit connectors; others prefer implicit connection
- Formal writing may require different connectors than speech
- Some connectors trigger grammatical changes (subjunctive, etc.)

### Additional Tagging for B-Level

B-level vocabulary benefits from extended tagging:

| Tag Category | Examples |
|--------------|----------|
| Register | formal, informal, neutral |
| Domain | business, legal, medical, academic |
| Grammar trigger | subjunctive, conditional |
| Frequency | high, medium, low |
| Collocation | verb+noun, adj+noun |

### Phrase Guidelines for B-Level

- Phrases can be slightly longer (6-8 words) for complex concepts
- Include professional/formal context sentences
- Show connectors in natural argumentative flow
- Demonstrate register-appropriate usage

## Summary

Effective B1/B2 vocabulary decks should:

1. **Target 350 words at B1** organized into 7 thematic decks
2. **Target 350 additional words at B2** organized into 7 thematic decks
3. **Progressively integrate professional vocabulary** across B1 and B2
4. **Develop abstract vocabulary** with proper contextualization
5. **Master discourse markers and connectors** for coherent argumentation
6. **Include idiomatic expressions** for natural fluency
7. **Teach full formal register** by B2 completion
8. **Align vocabulary with grammar progression** (subjunctive, conditionals)
9. **Note collocations and word partnerships** throughout
10. **Mark register** (formal/informal/neutral) for all entries

This framework provides the structure; language-specific research fills in the vocabulary and identifies the most frequent/useful items in each category.
